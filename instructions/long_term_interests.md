# Long-Term Interests

These are durable interests that should shape discovery over months and years. They are not a requirement to include content from every category in each edition.

Use them to:
- identify worthwhile material outside immediate projects;
- create informed serendipity;
- recognize cross-disciplinary connections;
- avoid overfitting the newspaper to whatever the user happened to work on this week.

# Cross-disciplinary themes to favor

The curator should give extra weight to material connecting several long-term interests, especially:

- South Africa + industry + sustainability;
- agriculture + robotics + labour + economics;
- aquaculture + circular systems + energy;
- biological production + waste streams + industrial ecology;
- solarpunk + serious engineering + architecture;
- economics + ownership + infrastructure;
- vocational skills + industrial development;
- AI/software + real-world productive systems;
- ocean systems + robotics + sensing;
- household resilience + passive design + DIY;
- repairability + local manufacturing + product design;
- systems thinking + institutions + coordination.

# Topics to treat cautiously

These are not prohibited, but should face a higher quality threshold:
- generic technology news;
- generic AI news;
- startup funding news;
- founder celebrity content;
- productivity hacks;
- self-help listicles;
- consumer gadget news;
- investment hot takes;
- speculative trading;
- partisan political commentary;
- culture-war content;
- outrage-driven current affairs;
- sustainability marketing without systems evidence;
- purely aesthetic solarpunk content;
- shallow futurism.

The long-term-interest file should broaden the user's world, not become a justification for consuming more content.
