Fix my own problem of content consumption by creating alternative algorithm.

- Follow the CEO of Patreon's vision for a more connection and wellbeing focused algorithm.

For curating my own digital diet

- Mostly reddit, linkedin, substack, & twitter (maybe Youtube)
- Feeds into AI composed newspaper

Way to better curate your digital diet and environment.

- Filter for what comes in
- Limits & priorities for your well-being; not screentime / ad-revenue
- Daily newspaper idea is a subset: AI to manage follows & recommendations.
- Friend recommendations power the discovery

- People have no easy way to control their digital enviornments = has a massive impact on your life and requires direct intervention, but is extremely hard and most won't do it.
- Serendipity and discovery should be social and not governed by an algorithm that is optimising for screentime.
- Lack of trust in the world, "what is real? What is true?"
- Less connection to creation by real people, more AI summaries. No one is reading the original sources.
- Readwise Reader people hoarding information and not being able to manage everything

## Plain language explanation

I’m struggling with information diet. I would like to build something that can [at least for written content] help a brother to curate and customize their digital diet. Basically trying to take back control of the algorithm.

The premise is basically that you have an ai that curates a daily ‘newspaper’ for you. So it preselects a bunch of articles or news pieces etc, based on predefined criteria, then dedupes, trims out noise and salient content, and provides you with an epub of probably 10-20 pieces that have been selected for reading. The main thing is that you’d ideally have an interface that keeps a record of these epubs (which if you didn’t know is just a package of html documents), and you can give feedback to the recommender on the articles. Basically the recommendation algorithm pulls from a bunch of platforms, blogs and news sites, and it optimizes for the well-being of the user.
